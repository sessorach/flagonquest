__MOVEMENT RULES ADJUST__

Ok how to make easier to run \- should be single step, no secondary rolls\. How do other systems do it?

5E: While climbing or swimming, each foot of movement costs 1 extra foot \(2 extra feet in difficult terrain\), unless a creature has a climbing or swimming speed\. When you make a long jump, you cover a number of feet up to your Strength score if you move at least 10 feet on foot immediately before the jump\. When you make a standing long jump, you can leap only half that distance\. Either way, each foot you clear on the jump costs a foot of movement\.

At the GM’s option, climbing a slippery vertical surface or one with few handholds requires a successful Strength \(Athletics\) check\. Similarly, gaining any distance in rough water might require a successful Strength \(Athletics\) check\.

This rule assumes that the height of your jump doesn’t matter, such as a jump across a stream or chasm\. At your GM’s option, you must succeed on a DC 10 Strength \(Athletics\) check to clear a low obstacle \(no taller than a quarter of the jump’s distance\), such as a hedge or low wall\. Otherwise, you hit it\.

When you land in difficult terrain, you must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity \(Acrobatics\) check to land on your feet\. Otherwise, you land prone\.

__Solution:__

Climbing, Swimming: Each space you climb or swim across takes an extra 1 meter of movement\.

Jumping: You can jump as part of up \[Athletics Skill Total\] meters, or half that distance vertically\. Jumping vertically includes the distance covered by your ability to reach and pull yourself up, if necessary\. If the jump is farther than your Speed you can combine actions to make it, but until you complete the jump you may only take actions to continue moving\.

In some situations, the GM may require a successful Athletics check to climb, jump, or swim\. The difficulty for these checks is based on the situation, and failure means that the rest of the action is Countered, possibly with additional consequences\. For a jump that might take multiple actions, make a check for the first action, and if it succeeds you do not need to make any additional checks for that jump\.

<a id="_nxc5r96hypc"></a>__POISON RITUAL__

How should poisons be valued? This ties into the general value of gold and items and all that, which sounds like a pain in the ass\. 

It turns out it’s super easy\. Each stack of a standard debuff like Crippled effectively is worth like 2\.5 points; 

Level 1 \(create DC 9\): Concentration \+2, Potency 1

Level 2 \(create DC 11\): Concentration \+4, Potency 2

Level 3 \(create DC 13\): Concentration \+6, Potency 3

Level 4 \(create DC 15\): Concentration \+8, Potency 4

Level 5 \(create DC 17\): Concentration \+10, Potency 5

<a id="_e3o0xiwz08li"></a>__THE GOLD STANDARD__

Thus, the end result is that each poison is effectively “worth” a Technique of that Level\. So the Gold standard is that however much gold a consumable, one\-time effect costs relates to that\. 3 Gold per Level would mean Poisons cost 3/6/9/12/15, and their effects are basically that amount\. 

Extending this metric, 1 Gold literally \-> \+1 value\.
