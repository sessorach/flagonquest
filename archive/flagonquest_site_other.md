Tavern Brawler (Level 1)
A veteran of melees in taprooms, bars, and other places where booze flows and tempers run high, you are well-versed in fighting with just about any object that might be at hand.
Passive
: This Technique applies to you at all times.
Effects
: You have the following benefits:
Once per scene when an attack you make Downs a target, you may immediately imbibe an alcoholic drink as long as it is within easy reach. If you do, you gain 1 temporary Health which lasts for the scene

Passive
: This Technique applies to you at all times.

: You may change how you wield a close-range weapon, using the same action as though you were drawing or sheathing a weapon. Pranic Nourishment (Level 2)
You can tune your body into the metaphysical weave of your surroundings, sustaining yourself off of the energy of the world around you in addition to that which exists within all matter.
Passive
: This Technique applies to you at all times.
Effects

## Glossary  
*Source: `glossary.html` (Part 2)*

These are keywords, tags, types, and all sorts of terms that many rules and abilities refer to. If you see an Important Capitalized Term and don't know what it does, look here!
Rules Tags
These tags designate items and abilities that work a certain way. Each tag has its own rules and, usually, some unique function.
[Daily]
Daily abilities are expended when you use them, and you regain their use when you get a full night's rest. During extended periods of time, over multiple days, the GM may limit the use of these abilities to less than once per day.
[Encounter]
Encounter abilities are expended when you use them, and you regain their use when the scene ends and a new one begins. Encounter Techniques can be learned multiple times, and each time you learn it you gain an additional use of it.
[Fleeting]
At the end of your turn you remove one stack of each Fleeting effect you have. Like other duration-based effects, you only remove stacks that you didn't gain on that turn.
[Grenade]
Grenades are Acrobatics ranged weapons with a range of
3 × [Body]
, attacks using them are against the target's Dodge Defense, you may not Gamble on attacks with them, and they are consumed after the attack is made.
[Implement]
When you cast a Spell, you may choose one of your Implements and apply its effects to the Spell. Weapons and other items can be used as Implements if you are wielding or holding them, as appropriate.
[Poison]
If an attack with a poisoned weapon successfully causes a creature to lose Health, that creature is exposed to the Poison. Make an attack, adding the Poison's Concentration, against the target's Bodily Defense. If it hits, the target suffers the Poison's effects.
A Poison takes 2 AP to apply to a weapon. A weapon remains poisoned until the end of the scene or until it exposes a creature to its Poison. A poisoned piece of ammunition always loses its Poison after it is used to make an attack.
Fleeting effects applied by Poisons do not expire normally. Instead, every hour 1 stack is removed, and these stacks
may not be removed any other way
.
[Potion]
A Potion can be consumed to provide the imbiber with an effect. It takes 1 AP to use a Potion.
[Re-Learning]
Abilities with this tag can be learned more than once, and some have special features based on how many times you learn them.
[Stance]
You may enter or leave a Stance at the start of your turn, before anything else happens. You may only be in one Stance at a time, and entering a Stance will cause you to leave any other Stance you are in. If the Stance lists a condition, you may only enter or gain the Stance's effects while you meet that condition.
Common Effects
These are positive and negative effects that many abilities and items apply. They make up many of the special effects in the game, and many items and abilities interact with them. Most of them have the Fleeting tag.
Bleeding [Fleeting]
When you remove a stack of Bleeding at the end of your turn, lose 1 Health.
Bolstered [Fleeting]
You ignore up to 1 Wound penalty you have.
Crippled [Fleeting]
You suffer a -1 penalty to attacks.
Frightened [Fleeting]
You have Bad Luck on any actions targeting the creature who Frightened you.
While you are Frightened, you cannot be Taunted.
Harried [Fleeting]
You suffer a -1 penalty to Dodge and Parry Defense. At the end of your turn, remove all stacks of Harried you have.
Hasted [Fleeting]
You gain a +1 bonus to Speed.
Necrotic [Fleeting]
If you would heal any Health, instead for each stack of Necrotic you have you reduce that amount by 1 and remove a stack of Necrotic. If you ever have stacks of both Necrotic and Protected, remove 1 of each until one effect is gone.
Protected [Fleeting]
If you would lose any Health, instead for each stack of Protected you have you prevent 1 Health being lost. Then you remove a stack of Protected for each Health loss prevented. This does not apply to Health paid as a cost.
Slowed [Fleeting]
You suffer a -1 penalty to Speed.
Taunted [Fleeting]
You have Bad Luck on any actions which do not target the creature who Taunted you.
If the creature who Taunted you is incapacitated or otherwise removed from combat, if that creature Frightens you, or if another creature would Taunt you, first remove all stacks of Taunted from yourself.
Vulnerable [Fleeting]
You suffer a -1 penalty to Resistance and Mental Defenses.
Fire/Frost/Brilliant/Shadow Ward [Fleeting]
You have +1 Resist against the specified type of damage.
Type Tags
These are tags that appear on many items and abilities. They do not have any rules associated with them, but some things interact specifically with certain tags listed here.
[Alcohol]
Alcohol, for rules purposes, refers to full drinks such as the entries under Alchemical Items.
[Animal]
Animals are completely normal, non-human creatures.
[Discipline]
Disciplines are mystical and meditative effects, usually the result of harnessing one's energies.
(Level X)
Indicates that the item or ability is of X Level. If multiple Levels are listed then the item or ability can be found or learned as any of the listed Levels, with effects depending on the Level.
[Martial]
Martial abilities include swordsmanship, karate, and every kind of fighting with a weapon.
[Social]
Social abilities are specific conversational tactics, usually used in social contests.
[Spell]
Spells are magical incantations and uses of magic that do a variety of things. All Spells also have a more specific type, including Elementalism Spells, Theurgy Spells, and General Spells.
Other Keywords
These are assorted rules terms that serve a range of functions.
Countered
A Technique, attack, or other ability that is Countered is still expended as normal, as are any other resources spent to use it, but otherwise has no effect as though it were never used.
Difficult Terrain
Sometimes conditions or features of terrain can give it a level of difficult terrain. In an area with a level of Difficult Terrain, a character takes two meters of Speed to cross one meter of terrain, meaning you move half as fast across it.
If there is more than one level of Difficult Terrain in a space, due to multiple effects or the like, each meter of that terrain instead takes (1 + [levels of Difficult Terrain]) meters of movement to cross.
Pushing
When you are pushed you are forcibly moved a certain distance, although this does not count as "moving". If a creature or object is in your way you are pushed through their space, and if the Push would result in you being in an occupied space you end up in the nearest unoccupied space instead. If the creature or object is particularly big you may instead be pushed against them, with various consequences.
Shift
Shifting is like moving, with two exceptions. Nobody may take any Interrupt actions to the movement, and when you Shift you ignore Difficult Terrain.
Summoned
A summoned creature can be given instructions, but otherwise will simply follow you around. In combat a Summoned creature does not act or gain Action Points on its own, but you may have it use your AP to take actions. For all other purposes it considers your turn to be its own.
Teleport
When you teleport you disappear from your current location and reappear at your destination without moving through any other spaces. To Teleport you must be able to clearly see your destination.

## Home  
*Source: `home.html` (Part 1)*

In Flagonquest you play as a character you get to design, choosing from a wide variety of fantasy-themed special abilities to represent your character's talents. Your character is not limited to a class or archetype, and instead you may mix and match anything you like. As your character grows in strength and experience you can continue to enhance your existing abilities, or have their adventures change their path as they learn entirely new abilities!
Flagonquest is played with a full standard deck of 54 playing cards: an Ace, 2-10, Jack, Queen, and King in each of four suits, as well as a red joker and a black joker. Better hedge your bets - a streak of good (or bad) luck is bound to balance out as you get near the end of your deck of cards!
Your Character
A character is represented by 3 key characteristics - Stats, Skills, and Techniques.
Stats
are traits such as Body and Mind which represent inherent parts of a character. As a character grows stronger and wiser, their Stats might increase to show they're made of tougher stuff than the average townsfolk.
Skills
are specific knowledges and arts that one practices and trains. Swordplay, magical arts, and persuasion are just some of the Skills that characters can develop to best the challenges they face.
Techniques

Legend Binding
Binding: Legend of {Item} (Level 1-5)
Every hero has legends of their armaments, tools, and every manner of equipment. As your legend grows, so too does your assortment of... stuff.
Passive
: This Technique applies to you at all times.
Effects
: When you learn this Technique, choose an item you own. You always treat this item as being in a convenient location, no matter where it is. No other creature may take ownership of this item, and it cannot be destroyed; instead, you are always able to simply reveal the item as being in your possession.
If the item is consumed on use, like a Potion or Grenade, this does not prevent the item from being consumed. Instead, this Technique applies to any given one item of the same kind as the chosen item. Additionally, whenever you would be separated from your equipment (like if you die) you keep the chosen item with you, and no other creature may consume or destroy that item.
Special
: This Technique's Level is equal to that of the chosen item. Instead of its usual Glory cost, this Technique costs Glory equal to its Level.

PREREQUISITE QUEST
Prerequisites are equal parts barrier and justification.
Barrier
: Keep anyone from freely accessing techniques, so that people have to choose carefully; make the choices more meaningful. To this end, prereqs reflect how 'special' certain mechanics are and force characters to invest to obtain the more special/powerful ones.
Justification
: Require that characters embody the narrative aspects that techniques would suggest, so that the abilities "fit" the characters. This is mostly reflected in the secondary/tertiary requirements, which should 'flavor' the prereqs and come from non-standard aspects of the Technique.
So for example, Healing Magic is just bread-and-butter Theurgy, so it doesn't really require anything else. The nature magic spells require the same level of Theurgy, but also require some Survival to reflect the additional aspect.

LEVEL
Technique level and what it means
Level 1
Simple, niche utility. 3xp = ~6 gold, so something minor that can come up a lot or something useful that comes up every few sessions.
Level 2
The cornerstone of a character, solid but simple. Encounters do a cool thing, passives are neat but minor.
Level 3
Strong ability (especially since this is where they start being worth more than one session's XP). A good step above, only need a few of these to have a big impact on a character.
Level 4
Effective capstone for a concept, save for the Level 5 cases. Some more mixed characters might not even get any of these, and just mix it between lower Level things. But the ones that do get powerful abilities that trivialize some lesser concerns and do some pretty strong stuff. Basically the best combat spells, big-hitter attacks, and abilities that let you use your favorite Skill to solve a half-dozen things. These are the best abilities out there.
Level 5
Basically breaking the rules. S-tier stuff, eclipse "the best abilities out there" by virtue of offering unparalleled mechanics and other things that simply don't exist outside this tier. Insanely strong, heavy requirements, focused effect. This is dumping a brick of XP (literally enough to max out a Skill from 0) into something to say "screw everyone I am the literal best". Actually maybe game-shatteringly good, perfect defenses and stuff. Can revisit the 5/5/5 requirement standard, but it should stay pretty high. These are something a character
only
picks up because it aligns very well with their concept.
Conceptual buckets
Character strengths are defined by 3 major categories - Strong, Spell, and Skillful.
These categories are split into 3 subcategories each
Strong
Strong includes both Body and Agility abilities, as well as a handful of those related to the Meditation Skill. Abilities that represent strong physical prowess, and all that accompanies that, can be found here. For some limited aspects, this can include mental strength as well; usually Integrity-related. Strong abilities feel reliable and solid, easily forming the backbone of a character's power.
Tactical
covers most attack, dodge, and parry related uses of Acrobatics, Archery, Melee, and Brawl. Depending on the fighting style it can cover many different tactics, but always with a deliberate calculation of offense versus defense, power versus precision, with a default emphasis on being well-rounded. Tactical abilities will generally make characters feel as though they are outmaneuvering their opponents, making a
tactical
choice at each step.
Signature Skills
: Acrobatics, Archery, Melee, Brawl
Martial Arts
provides specific uses for Brawl and Meditation, and helps Meditation serve as a sort of 'third caster skill'. The subcategory includes a number of martial schools, each with differing focuses, but all of which are distinct from Tactical options. Where Tactical abilities represent more reactive and analytical strategies (on average), MA abilities represent "buy-in" to a certain strategy - Great Old Oak Style's defensive approach, Demon Style's reckless, aggressive approach, and so on. As such, MA abilities let characters feel ownership over a certain style and approach that shows how they fight.
Signature Skills
: Brawl, Meditation
Overpowering
is in part a catch-all category, but it has a solid unifying theme of simply
being very strong
. It's in a way the flagship subcategory of the Strong category, in that it represents overpowering physical ability. Overpowering abilities vary a bit depending on which Skill they're related to, but characters feel truly powerful when using them for their favorite Skill.
Signature Skills
: Might, Resilience, Acrobatics, Athletics, Awareness
Savvy
Savvy includes many knowledge-based abilities, both academic and practical. Savvy abilities are flexible and ingenious, allowing a character's favorite Skill(s) to be used in many situations. Savvy abilities feel like they give you more creative ways to solve problems, and make you "better" at a variety of useful things.
Expert
is the exemplar group in Savvy, and its theme is being good and knowledgeable about a thing - as in, objects, not people. Expert abilities empower a character and make their Skills more useful and applicable, but their flexibility has a cost - the more broadly useful an ability is, the more setup it requires. A character with Expert abilities feels like they get a lot of mileage out of their Skills.
Signature Skills
: Stealth, Masquerade, Survival, Medicine, Streetwise, Academics, Craft,  Mixology
Willful
abilities represent a character's ability to force the world to obey them. Strength of will, a sharp mind, and discipline (not the martial arts kind lmao) lets a character push themselves beyond normal limits and reap unique benefits, but might require sacrifice. A Willful character feels different and impressive in their own, special way.
Signature Skills
: Integrity, (Misc.)
Personality
includes all social and interpersonal abilities. While some abilities in other categories involve interacting with people (such as Masquerade), Personality abilities provide ways to manipulate and inspire others specifically. Characters well-versed in them feel strong and capable whenever they have other people to work with and recruit to their cause.
Signature Skills

## Meta Book  
*Source: `meta-book.html` (Part 2)*

Meta Book
Basics of Roleplaying
As a tabletop
roleplaying
game, the idea of F&F is to take the role of a character you create and embark on fantastic adventures as that character. When you play the game, you are playing
as
the character you have made, and every decision you make should be an extension of that character and in no way concern you as the player.
In the most basic sense, the game is about each player making a character and playing/acting as that character in a world, with situations presented by the Game Master - also known as the GM. These situations usually come about in the course of the characters working towards a goal: completing a quest or mission given to them by someone, achieving or obtaining something for personal reasons, or any number of other motivations. In these situations, the group of players - known as the party - must decide their characters' actions based on their motivations, personalities, and other factors. As circumstances become more complicated, the party may find individual members at odds, and the whole group being forced to make difficult decisions.
Even if you, as a person, have a piece of knowledge, it doesn't mean your character would; likewise, if your character has particularly impressive knowledge about a subject they may be privy to information you might not personally know. As you are stepping into the mind of your character, and are making decisions appropriately. When you design your character and their personality, you are choosing how you are going to play the game through that character. If you create a character that has very little Intelligence or related Skills then you will have much less knowledge to act off of, if you make a brash and impulsive character then you should deliberate very little before acting, and if you make a character that strives to solve problems nonviolently then you should hesitate before resorting to combat. Every choice you make is on behalf of your character, through their mind, with their knowledge and morals.
As an extension of this, there are two golden rules of roleplaying in a format like F&F:
No "metagaming"
: When you use information that you, the player, possess outside of your character's mind, it is referred to as 'metagaming', information about the game but outside of it. Always do your best to act
only
on the knowledge your character possesses, and you should do your best to not act on anything you (as the player) know that your character does not.
Keep your morals and politics in-character
: If you create a character that has very strong beliefs about a subject, it should be understood by everyone playing that these beliefs belong to the character and do not reflect your beliefs in any way. Any player is welcome to play a character with as radical beliefs as they choose (assuming it fits the overall theme of the game) and while some subject matter might be deemed distasteful by the group at large nobody should take isssue
outside of the game
with a particular belief or action of a character. If two characters are at odds because of disparate beliefs, this is fine - and can even make for some very compelling roleplaying - but this disagreement should never extend
outside of the game
.
Generally, gameplay (outside of combat, which has more specific rules) will follow this pattern:
The GM describes the current scene, including the characters' surroundings, what's going on, and any other relevant details
For each character who is present in the scene, their players decide what they want their characters to do, and how they'd like to try to achieve it
The GM decides what the results of the characters' actions are, which may necessitate card flips from either the players or the GM to determine outcomes
The GM describes how the outcomes of various actions affect the scene, and what the overall scenario is as a result
As before, the players decide what their characters' actions will be, the GM adjudicates what happens, and the cycles continues until a new scene begins
In all situations, whatever the GM says goes, even if it might contradict what is normally the case, however especially in those sorts of situations there's never any harm in making sure that everyone is on the same page. If a player thinks that the GM might be mistaken, either about a particular rule, their character's intent, a fact about the game, or anything similar, they should feel free to mention it to the GM and clarify that everyone shares an understanding. That being said, ultimately the GM has the final word on matters related to the game.

## New Ideas  
*Source: `new-ideas.html` (Part 2)*

New Ideas
The idea is to simplify the rules as much as possible while keeping to a few principles:
There should be a variety of options for a given scenario
There should be some difference between options presented
If two options exist they should either be distinct from a player decision standpoint or they should be combined into one option
The different options and their consequences/complications should be relatively obvious - no "gotcha!" moments that aren't unexpected
The goal here should be that as many rules as possible are removed from the list of "necessary reading" - only necessary if you have the Technique, and otherwise interacting with as few rules as possible (Defenses, etc.)
POWER TYPE THEORY
Framework in which to organize Techniques. No real mechanical impact, except as a way to group and distribute Techniques with consciousness of what "types" of characters will have access to these abilities.
Magical
: Most obvious group, literally things that are Spells. The easiest group to include things in, since magic doesn't have to make sense, but should be very careful and give it clear limits. It should not allow Elementalism/Theurgy to be used in place of other Skills, although it will likely share some functionalities the whole point is that you shouldn't be able to grab one Skill and have it completely be used for another.
Skillful
: Not to be confused with Skills, although closely tied to them in particular. Deliberate usage, for the most part, enhancements to Skills and other niche ways to use them. Being so good at something that you defy logic and common sense.
Strong
: Distinguished from Skillfull in that these are just physical traits turned up to 11, and while they
might
be active use the general pattern is that they're passive capabilities that come to light in relevant situations. Being so strong that you ignore half the encumbrance rules, being able to eat broken glass and not wince, blindsight, stuff like that.
So how do we split things up? There are lots of Techniques that clearly only fit into one - throwing fireballs is Magical, being a perfect surgeon is Skillful, and being so tough that you digest poison when you eat it is Strength. You can start to make justifications for how a Technique could have similar versions across power types, but it's contrived. More broad mechanics can easily find homes in multiple power types though: healing magic can cure poisons, a very good doctor can cure poisons, and you can be so strong you flex poison out of your body. For the example of poison and similar things especially, it's fitting to have each "type" of character have options for dealing with it.
Instances where a Technique/mechanic is present for two types but not a third should be more specifically cases of trying to
exclude
that from a power type. Deliberately keeping any reusable attack from Magic (except placeholder's torch which is clearly a meme) is about making sure those Skills aren't skeleton keys, but even this is about the Skills and not types. Really, very few things I can think of are present in two types but not the third; they're either specifically for one, or universal, Fireballs or Healing.

A Little Bit of Math
In a good number of Technique descriptions and assorted rules you will see a little bit of arithmetic repeated:
([Result] - 7) / 2
. Doing that mental math constantly can get a bit confusing, so an easy way to remember what it does is that after you make the flip, you count every 2 that flip got past a result of 7. So if your result on the flip was 7, it would just be 0; if your result on the flip was 9, it would be 1; if your result on the flip was 11, it would be 2; this pattern continues on. After a while, you can just remember that whenever you see that little bit of math it means that if you got a 13 that means 3 and skip having to grab a napkin and a pen.

Stunting
Sometimes a player does a really great job of roleplaying an encounter in a way that draws in everyone at the table, and they deserve to be rewarded. Stunting encourages players to contribute to the atmosphere of the game and rewards players for staying in character when making decisions, even to their detriment. It is important to emphasize that the purpose of stunting is to encourage players to bring flavor to the game by doing particularly interesting things, making difficult and complicated choices and fleshing out your character; it is not for simply using a bunch of pretty words. The goal is to contribute to the atmosphere at the table, not just to show off your new thesaurus.
When the GM decides that someone is taking an action that's particularly interesting or well-done, they can give that player a bonus. This might be a small +1 or +2 bonus to the flip in question, Advantage on that flip, or even allowing the player to draw a card.
Example
: Steve describes in detail how Rugged Ben tells the story of when an old farmer accidentally got some Western Centipede Venom on his hands and didn't get it washed off right quick; poor fellow was dead within the hour. Rugged Ben then offhandedly mentions how it's surprisingly easy to procure some of the venom and trails off a bit... then shoots a pointed look at the drunkard he was chatting with in the tavern and asks him again, politely, if he knows anything about the man who was killed in the alleyway last night. After the table stops laughing, the GM tells Steve that he has Advantage on the Intimidate flip.

Destroying Objects
Actual objects are harder to destroy than people are to kill, generally speaking.
Most objects are grouped into categories based on their component materials and construction:
Delicate
: 0 Natural Soak, 0 Armor Soak
Normal
: 2 Natural Soak, 2 Armor Soak
Sturdy
: 4 Natural Soak, 4 Armor Soak
Impenetrable
: 6 Natural Soak, 6 Armor Soak
A pane of glass would likely be a "delicate" object, a wooden chair would be "normal", a stone sculpture "sturdy", and thick iron bars would be considered "impenetrable".
Likewise, the size of the object determines how much Health the object has:
Small
: 5 Health
Medium
: 10 Health
Large
: 15 Health
When an object's Health is reduced to 0 it is damaged to the point of being inoperable, although not necessarily completely destroyed.

For Skills in particular, the following is a rough guideline for what each rating means in abstract terms:
0:
You have no training or knowledge beyond a few scraps of common knowledge. You might know which end of a sword is the handle, but you've never actually practiced fighting with one.
1:
You have a base level of competency and training. You might have survived enough street fights to learn how to throw a punch, or maybe you just finished basic training to be eligible for military service, but either way you know how to fight, if only at a basic level.
2:
You are officially practiced and knowledgeable in the skill. At this level you can call yourself professionally skilled in your craft, and just about any soldier who serves in more than a few scuffles will have this level of skill.
3:
Congratulations, at this point you are officially good. You have devoted a substantial amount of time and effort getting to this level of skill, or you happen to have considerable natural talent. With this level of magical knowledge one can begin to effect actual spells, and martial prowess at this level belongs to veteran soldiers, grizzled old mercenaries, and the like.
4:
You are among the elite in your chosen skill. In a room full of people odds are nobody is your equal, your knowledge of the subject is deep and nuanced, and if you are a professional you may be sought out for your expertise alone. A military leader with a long successful career and natural talent is at this level, or an archer who has spent his life perfecting the art.
5:
A consummate master with total understanding of the skill. Wizards with the skill to cast even the most taxing spells, grandmasters of the blade who are considered to have complete mastery over their styles, and legendary bards who compose songs that will be sung for centuries are all examples of people at this skill level.
Note, however, that this is generally how the skill levels are for most people. As PCs, you tend to be people who are exemplary and outside the norm. While there might only be a handful of people in a small army that have 5 points in their relevant combat skill, odds are very high that some or most of them are PCs. If you do something for a living, you probably only need 1-2 points in it, maybe 3 in extreme cases, to represent that level of knowledge or skill.
Magic users skilled in the art of artifice are capable of working with magically-conductive materials to create items that channel magic by themselves, allowing for a variety of astounding effects ranging from armor that shields its wearer from flames to wands that allow those untrained relatively untrained in magic to, with some effort, duplicate magical spells. The effects of enchanted items are as numerous as those who create them, but a general list follows to give an idea of what is possible.

Popcorn Initiative
When combat becomes inevitable and everyone begins to reach for their weapons,
initiative
is rolled to determine who acts the fastest. Each combatant rolls 1d10 and adds their Initiative Modifier to the roll; the first
combat round
starts, and whoever rolls the highest on their Initiative roll takes their turn first.
When a combat round starts, every combatant is placed in a pool. When a combatant begins their turn they are removed from this pool; after a combatant finishes their turn, they choose someone from the pool to take the next turn. This continues until the last combatant in the pool has taken their turn, at which point a new round begins, all remaining combatants are placed back in the pool, and the last combatant chooses someone from the pool to take the next turn, which now includes every combatant including themself.
If one or more combatants are unaware that combat is starting - a group is ambushed by bandits in the woods, for example - then combat begins with a
surprise round
. The pool of available combatants for this round
only
includes those who are aware that combat is imminent, and after this pool has acted then combat continues as normal with every remaining combatant taking a turn.
An exception to the Initiative order may be made when there are only two remaining combatants, in which case turn order merely alternates until the combat is ended.

Note that this does not apply to weapons already made from wood or the like, such as bows or clubs.
Stone/Bone
: Weapons may have metal parts replaced with stone, such as a spear with a stone point or an axe with a stone head. It is generally impractical to incorporate stone in armor construction although there are some exceptions to this, such as a sort of "banded mail" with rocks lashed over leather. As a commonly found material, many weapons and armor are made from bone when metal is scarce, and as such most weapons and armor can have bone substituted as a material.  Weapons and armor that are made of stone in place of metal have -1 Accuracy, -1 Defense, has its Max Dex rating reduced by 2 (minimum Max Dex of 1), and has its Might Pool and Endurance pool Requirements increased by 1.

What should a stance do?
My favorite stance is probably Half-Guard - You gain a +1 bonus to Parry Defense, and your Speed is halved. It is a minor bonus, but also a powerful one; it's something that isn't usually available through abilities, has a nice impact, but it comes at a rather inconvenient cost. If this were free everyone might take it, but with a bit of an XP cost (5 here) it ends up only really being useful to certain characters.
Overchanneling is another one, to the point where some things are kind of balanced with it in mind. Mechanically it works in-line with other things (weapon damage table), allowing you to use your spells like a 2-hand weapon basically, and thematically it lets you go all-in on offense, which is kind of a neat thing. Would having this as a built-in option be too good? Once again, putting a moderate XP cost on the ability feels appropriate.
So from these two examples, there are a couple of elements to this:
The benefit of the stance should be rather good, important enough to the character that they will make sacrifices for it beyond just XP
OR
the stance's effect should be niche and un-abusable enough that it needs no drawback
If the stance has a drawback, it should be a sensible counterweight to the stance's benefit
ALSO
, it should only be dramatic enough to prevent characters from using the stance unless it fits their concept / strategy to a certain degree
Additionally, there is the question of how much the "stance slot" is worth. There is an inherent drawback in stances, because you must choose to only be in one at a time, so their effects should be solid enough that they are worth using over things like Half Guard and Overchanneling; that is, assuming that their effects mesh with what you want to do. Basically, the idea is that if there is a stance that supports your style of combat, there shouldn't be another option that you use
strictly because it's better in the objective sense
. This doesn't mean that it's a bad idea to take a stance to shore up a weakness, or anything, but instead that if someone is trying to make an archer but they don't take the ranged stances and instead take some other stance
for general purposes
because it's just that good, then either that stance is too good or the archery ones aren't good enough.
In a nutshell, all stances should be compared against a baseline of something like Half Guard to make sure that
in their niche
they are at least comparable.

Framework in which to organize Techniques. There isn't an inherent mechanical impact of these groups, but each group will have access to certain mechanics but not others.

## Stats and Skills  
*Source: `stats-and-skills.html` (Part 1)*

Stats are general measures of your inherent qualities. These can be improved over time, but they represent fundamental parts of your being that can manifest in different ways. The five Stats are
Agility
,
Body
,
Instinct
,
Mind
, and
Spirit
.
Skills represent your abilities as applied to more specific situations. These are more deliberately improved as you increase in experience, and usually involve knowledge and training rather than inherent ability.
Agility
Agility measures a character's flexibility, quickness, and manual dexterity. It impacts a character's ability to dodge and strike, balance and move nimbly, act silently and undetected, and more. Characters with more Agility are precise, careful, and elusive. Agility determines your Speed. Agility corresponds to the following Skills:
Acrobatics
Balancing, gymnastics, and other finesse-based exercises. It can be used to determine Dodge Defense and attack with thrown weapons.
Archery
Marksmanship with bows and similar armaments. It can be used to attack with bows.
Brawl
All forms of unarmed combat, including grappling and wrestling, as well as the use of some special weapons. It can be used to determine Dodge and Parry Defense and attack with unarmed attacks and some other weapons.
Melee
Close-quarters combat with melee weapons. It can be used to determine Parry Defense and attack with melee weapons.
Stealth
Sneaking, hiding, and generally avoiding notice. Whenever you are trying to keep silent, stay out of sight, or do something without someone seeing or hearing you, Stealth comes into play.
Body
Body represents how strong and tough a character is. Anything that involves raw brawn or resilience will be impacted by a character's Body. Characters with high Body are powerful and hard to stop. Body determines your damage with many weapons. Body corresponds to the following Skills:
Athletics
Climbing, jumping, swimming, and other strength-based exercises. Whenever you are engaged in a physical feat that relies on your raw strength, Athletics is what you use.
Awareness
See, hear, or otherwise sense details and objects that might not be immediately apparent. Whenever you are trying to spot someone who is hiding from you, you are searching through a room for clues, or otherwise trying to find or notice something physical, Awareness determines how well you do.
Might
Being very strong and carrying a lot of weight. It is required to use certain armaments and determines how much you can carry.
Presence
Impress and awe others with your bearing and stature, for both intimidating them and commanding them. It can be used to make Forceful arguments in social contests.
Resilience
Resist the adverse effects of poison, disease, exhaustion, and many other harmful bodily threats. It is used to determine Bodily Defense.
Instinct
Instinct is the sum of a character's subconscious senses and mental reaction time. It affects a character's gut feeling, interactions with others that involve reading their reactions, and knowledge that requires a feeling for the situation. Characters with a lot of Instinct are constantly reading their surroundings to stay a step ahead of everyone else. Instinct determines how many cards you draw each morning, as well as the damage of a select few weapons. Instinct corresponds to the following Skills:
Insight
Sense the motives of others, looming danger, and other things that are insubstantial or not directly perceivable. It represents a sort of "sixth sense", and determines your Reflex bonus as well as your Suspicion Defense in social contests.
Masquerade
Disguise yourself, impersonate another person, mimic a pattern of speech, or otherwise pretend to be someone else. Whenever you are trying to specifically change your behavior or appearance to seem to be a different person, you adopt a Masquerade.
Persuasion
Convince others of what you say, regardless of the truth of your statements. Whether you are trying to mislead someone or convince them that this ridiculous rumor is actually true, it all relies on your ability of Persuasion. It is also used to make Maneuvering and Debate arguments and determines your Debate Defense in social contests.
Streetwise
Knowledge of cities and their various social structures as well as your ability to exploit it in any given situation. Whether in a seedy Thieves' Guild tavern or a high nobility ballroom, your Streetwise allows you to stay on top of the shifting power hierarchies and know who to talk to in order to get things done.
Survival
Knowledge of nature and survival as well as your ability to apply that knowledge in the middle of the wilderness. More than just knowing which plants are edible, it allows you to read the land like a book and learn all about it.
Mind
Mind is a character's ability to learn, retain, and apply information in both academic and practical settings. It reflects the amount of knowledge a character has absorbed from a variety of disciplines, as well as the extent to which a character can apply that information in a range of situations. Characters with a strong Mind can leverage their encyclopedic knowledge and mental training to solve any problem. Mind determines your maximum Focus and increases the damage of many spells. Mind corresponds to the following Skills:
Academics
Knowledge of history, mathematics, literature, and other subjects frequently thought of as "book learning". Generally, Academics is used to recall most knowledge that is not covered by other Skills.
Craft
Understanding of woodworking, metalworking, and other disciplines by which you create armaments and other objects. It is used both to create these items yourself and to better manipulate existing objects, such as disabling a tripwire or picking a lock.
Integrity
Resist fear, compulsion, and other mental manipulations both mundane and magical. It is used to determine Mental Defense.
Medicine
Understanding of the body and how certain treatments can heal or harm it. It is mostly used to care for others and tend to their injuries and maladies, including poison and disease, although there are more mercenary uses for this knowledge as well.
Mixology
Understanding of chemical and alchemical principles and how to combine reagents to produce a variety of potions, grenades, and other goods. It is used primarily for the creation and identification of alchemical goods.
Spirit
Spirit is the measure of a character's metaphysical strength and conditioning. This manifests in the character's presence, ability to connect emotionally with other creatures, and better govern their body's energies. Characters with a powerful Spirit are better in tune with others and with their own bodies, and can push themselves to superhuman heights. Spirit determines your Natural Resist. Spirit corresponds to the following Skills:
Elementalism
Wield destructive elemental magic to blast your enemies and perform impressive magical feats. It also represents your knowledge of practical magic use and can help you identify magical effects.
Meditation
Tap into your spiritual energies to bolster your vitality and strike out at your enemies. Many formal martial arts make use of Meditation, in small or large ways, and some dramatic uses of it can allow you to fire bursts of energy to defeat your foes.
Performance
Put on any sort of entertaining or inspiring performance, both with instruments and spoken words. Whether you are giving an encouraging speech to a crowd or breathing life into a tavern party with your lute music, you are putting on a Performance.
Rapport
Interact with people amicably, endear yourself to others, and generally be likable and friendly. It is used to make Maneuvering and Forceful arguments in social contests.
Theurgy
Manipulate subtle spiritual energies to aid your allies and keep your foes at bay. It also represents your knowledge of practical magic use and can help you identify magical effects.

## Stats and Skills  
*Source: `stats-and-skills_1.html` (Part 1)*

Stats and Skills are the core of your character's capabilities, representing strengths and areas in which your character has trained and learned. Stats represent your innate capabilities, and are broad-stroke measurements of your physical fitness, mental ability, and related concepts. Skills, on the other hand, represent specific training and areas in which you have practiced and improved yourself. Each Skill is listed under its corresponding Stat, and unless specifically stated a Skill is always used with its corresponding Stat.
Strength
A measure of your raw physical strength and power. Increases most weapon damage and thrown weapon distance.
Strength corresponds to these Skills:
Athletics
Jumping, swimming, climbing, and other feats of fitness that relate to physical power more than nimbleness and flexibility (which is covered by Acrobatics).
Might
Lifting, throwing, moving, and breaking heavy objects. All miscellaneous muscle-based activities that rely on sheer power.
Dexterity
Your flexibility, coordination, and physical agility. Useful for many Skills, especially combat-related ones, determining Dodge and Parry Defenses as well as Movement Speed.
Dexterity corresponds to these Skills:
Acrobatics
Tumbling, balancing, dodging, and other gymnastics that relate to nimbleness and flexibility more than physical (which is covered by Athletics). Used for Thrown weaponry, can be used for Dodge Defense.
Archery
Training in the use of bows and crossbows, as well as other aimed ranged weaponry. Focused on offense and deadly weaponry.
Legerdemain
Sleight of hand and other acts of fine manipulation and manual dexterity, including lockpicking, finding traps, disabling mechanical devices, and so on.
Martial Arts
Training in a style of unarmed combat, occasionally employing specific weapons as well. Versatile combat Skill, can be used for Dodge and Parry Defenses, well-rounded but with few specific strengths.
Melee
The most common of combat Skills, determining training in virtually all close-combat weapons. Can be used for Parry Defense.
Stealth
Your ability to avoid notice and stay hidden in general, whether simply moving through a crowd of people without making yourself conspicuous or skulking through shrubbery without being seen or making any noise.
Stamina
Your body's ability to resist disease, poison, and harm in general. Contributes to Natural Soak, Resistance Defense, natural healing rate, dying Health Levels, and other forms of resilience.
Stamina corresponds to only one Skill:
Resistance
Ability to weather physical hardship and trauma, avoid the effects of inclement weather and harsh conditions, go without sleep or food or water, withstand of poison or disease, and various physical ailments. Determines your Resistance Defense.
Cunning
Your mental agility, ability to think quickly, notice things, and apply practical knowledge on the fly. Relates to a great assortment of practical Skills and determines Initiative modifier.
Cunning corresponds to these Skills:
Awareness
Your ability to notice details, spot hidden things, and your overall sensory acumen. Straightforward uses of your basic senses and what you can sense using them.
Disguise
Determines how well you can craft a disguise, for yourself or someone else, and how convincing that disguise is. Some situations may require other Skills, such as Persuasion or Perform, to convince people you interact with that you are who you appear to be.
Insight
How well you can gain clues and deduce things that aren't immediately obvious from your surroundings, particularly piecing together information that you have learned. Governs how well you can sense others' motives, judge if people are being misleading, and pick up on behavioral clues.
Persuasion
Your knack for making other people think that what you say is true, whether it actually is or not, and win others over to your way of thinking. More about convincing people than actually making them like you (covered instead by Diplomacy).
Savvy
A combination of streetwise, knowing how to best gather information in a variety of locales, and underworld knowledge. With high Savvy you always know what's going on, or at least who to talk to in order to get up to speed.
Survival
Wilderness survival and general self-reliance while traveling, including your ability to navigate, find food and water, and identify plants and animals.
Intelligence
Your overall mental "power", measures your ability to learn and retain knowledge. Useful for a variety of Skills and knowledges, used for some spellcasting.
Intelligence corresponds to these Skills:
Academics
Your knowledge of a variety of scholarly pursuits, including history, engineering, economics, and many others. While many Skills cover knowledge of subjects related to their practical applications, Academics represents a more scholarly sort of learning about most topics not covered by other Skills.
Craft
Craft concerns the process of taking leather, metal, wood, and other materials and creating objects, such as weapons and armor, from them. Your score in the Skill represents both an ability to create items and a knowledge of their construction and the materials involved.
Elementalism
Your practical knowledge of Elementalism magic. Represents both your ability to cast Spells from that branch of magic as well as your overall knowledge of related magic.
Medicine
General and specific knowledge of the body, what can go wrong with it, and how to treat it. Covers both your knowledge of the steps to proper first aid as well as ability to perform surgery, and everything in between.
Mixology
Your capability of using precise measurement and mixture of ingredients to create everything from stews to wines to potions. Governs most acts of alchemy, cooking, brewing, and similar disciplines.
Will
Your conviction, mental fortitude, and ability to keep focused and avoid fear, seduction, curiosity, and other distractions. Determines Mental Defense, maximum Willpower, and the potency of a number of Spells.
Will corresponds to these Skills:
Centering
Your control over your body's spiritual and mental energies, meditation, and collecting your thoughts. Used for some Martial Arts and other related Techniques, functions mostly in specific applications similar to Occult or Prayer.
Integrity
How well-tempered your conviction is, measures your courage and ability to resist outside influences on your will and mental well-being. Determines your Mental Defense.
Charisma
Your personal charm, ability to connect with others emotionally, and overall force of personality. Useful for a variety of social skills, as well as working Theurgy magic.
Charisma corresponds to these Skills:
Diplomacy
Inspiring others, making friends, being likable in general. Rather than giving arguments for why someone should agree with you (which would be Persuasion), Diplomacy is used for calming others and making them like you more so that they might be more receptive to your words.
Intimidation
Ability to intimidate and cow others by sheer appearance and force of personality. While anyone can make a threat, those with higher Intimidation seem much more likely to carry out such a threat.
Perform
Governs all manner of entertainment, from music to storytelling to giving speeches. Represents a broad familiarity and ability with an assortment of instruments and mediums.
Theurgy
Your practical knowledge of Theurgy magic. Used for casting many Spells, including Theurgy magic, as well as identifying related magic
