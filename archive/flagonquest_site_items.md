Alcohol, Simple (weak ale, cheap wine, etc.)
Mankind has been fermenting and distilling alcohol since time immemorial, and as such it would be impossible to list all the possible alcohols. Nevertheless, those in this category are among the most basic and relatively weak of alcohols that one can make. That being said they do get the job done, and this includes some favorites such as ale one would find in a low-end tavern, the kind of wine that comes in a large stone jug and is intended to be finished in one sitting, and "whiskey" made from the most questionable of grains.
For better or worse, these alcohols are created and sold in volumes of about a liter.

Used in bookbinding and a wide variety of other pursuits, adhesives such as this are very convenient tools to have and as such are considered invaluable to the resourceful adventurer. Recreational Drugs, Poor Quality (Crystal meth, Spore, etc.)
There are drugs that are simply naturally-occurring plants that are smoked or ingested to provide a high, but there are also drugs that are manufactured by people. Generally this manufacturing process involves alchemy to refine the base substance(s) and remove impurities, but for drugs of this quality much of that is... overlooked. Either some aspect of the refining process was done improperly (or ignored), the base materials were contaminated or too poor, or the drug being created is simply a dangerous and harmful substance. Drugs of this "caliber" frequently form very strong habits, withdrawal can be lethal, and surviving long-term use will see adverse effects from the continued abuse. A list of common side-effects is as follows:
While under the effects of the drug, you are easily angered and tend to react to any adverse situation with violence. In any situation where someone stops you from getting what you want you must make an Integrity flip, Difficulty 11, or at the very least threaten violence. In the case that someone threatens you or otherwise suggests engaging in violence with you, you must spend 1 Soul or immediately attack them.
You are mentally unhinged to a certain degree, sometimes having difficulty distinguishing reality from... something else. You hear voices and phantom noises, catch visions of something otherworldly out of the corner of your eye, and sometimes you simply see something one moment only to blink and see a different scenario.
You immediately lose 1 Health that cannot be healed unless you are completely sobered from the influence of the drug.
For each month of continued use of the drug, your highest Health level has its maximum reduced by 1 until you go a month without using the drug (possible suffering withdrawal). This effect is cumulative with itself, and once a Health level has its maximum reduced to 0 your next highest Health level will begin to be affected. If this reduced your Incapacitated Health level to a maximum of 0, you die.

A smokejar consists of a stone or clay flask containing a small glass bubble, both of which contain alchemical reagents. When the glass bubble inside is shattered and the materials react with each other - usually accomplished by either shattering the jar on the ground or shaking it violently - a great amount of smoke billows out of the mixture. Initially the smoke covers a 1-meter-radius circular area centered on the jar, and at the start of the next 2 turns the radius increases by 1, eventually covering a 3-meter-radius area. The smoke hangs in the air for the scene or until dispersed by a strong wind.
In a pinch the jar can be thrown as a Grenade. When used for an attack choose one of the attack's targets; the jar breaks on that target and the smoke cloud is centered on them. If the attack hits, the target is Crippled twice for 1 round.

## Item Creation  
*Source: `item-creation.html` (Part 1)*

Creating items is generally done using the Craft or Mixology Skills, although in some situations other Skills might be used. In order to make an item, there are four things that you need: materials, tools, skill, and time.
Materials
Each item requires a certain number of raw materials to create. These materials must be at least the Level of the item you're creating. For each material Type the item has, you must include a raw material that matches that Type, but the same material can't count for two different Types.
If an item lists multiple Types as interchangeable, such as (Metal or Bone), then only one of those Types must be included, although you may include more than one.
Tools
In order to work the materials into an item you need proper tools, a proper workspace, and a recipe for the item. Some materials (like metal) might require a larger workshop to craft.
Skill
To create an item you require a certain level of skill. Each item will state its Required Skill, and how high your Skill Total must be in that Skill. You must have at least a Skill Total for the Required Skill of
[Item's Minimum Skill Total]
, as well as at least one rank in the Required Skill, to be able to create it.
Time
Creating an item takes time, depending on the item type, relevant Skill, and material used. Most Mixology items take no more than an hour to make, whereas many Craft items (especially ones made from metal) can take up to 8 hours to make.
Raw Materials
Raw materials have a Level and one or more Types. Most materials have one Type, some may have 2, but only the most rare and unique of materials have 3 or more. The material Types are:
Bone
: Bones, fangs, claws, scales, and similar material taken from an animal (or humanoid).
Brilliant
: Material aligned in a spiritually positive way.
Cloth
: Cotton, wool, linen, and other fabrics.
Fire
: Material aligned with fire, lightning, and other energetic elements.
Frost
: Material aligned with ice, cold, and other elements of stasis and stillness.
Leather
: Fur, hide, skin, and similar materials harvested from (hopefully dead) animals (or humanoids).
Medicinal
: Herbs, venom, fungus, and other natural materials harvested from plants and animals. Used in medicines, poisons, and everything in between.
Metal
: Iron, steel, copper, etc. Can include gemstone, if it can be properly worked into a new form.
Precious
: Gold, silver, silk, and any other rare, valuable material.
Shadow
: Material aligned in a spiritually negative way.
Wood
: Maple, oak, cedar, pine, and other material harvested from trees and similar plants.
Most raw materials are worth Gold equal to their Level. However, many materials only appeal to some tradespeople, and higher-quality materials can be hard to find for sale.

## Items and Equipment  
*Source: `items-and-equipment.html` (Part 1)*

Items and Equipment
Weapons, armor, potions, and a variety of other items and equipment are integral to your life when you're an adventurer, soldier, or even a savvy merchant who expects a bit of trouble from time to time.
Rather than listing prices or other uses of money in specific amounts, instead 'Gold' is used as a general unit of money. One Gold might be something like pouches of coins, a lump of precious metal, or a handful of valuable gemstones that can be used as payment, and they represent a significant amount of money. A character might spend a couple of coins on a mug of beer without really affecting how much money they have, and thus the GM may not assign a cost to that. However, if the character wants to buy a round of drinks for the whole bar then the GM might say that costs enough money that it has a price of 1 Gold, meaning the character would have to fork over enough money that it has a notable cost.
Each item entry will have some or all of these:
Item
- The item's name or names, including what it may be commonly referred to as.
Description
- A general description of the item, including any rules or mechanical effects the item has.
Average price
- The approximate cost of the item in units of money, under normal circumstances. Some items are bought in batches, listed after the price, meaning that for the item's price you can purchase a certain number of the item. For example, "(per 3x)" means that for that price you can purchase 3 of the item.
Relevant Skill
- the Skill used for creating the item.
Difficulty to make
- the Skill Total you need for the item's relevant Skill in order to create the item.
Body Slots: These are slots for specific items, for example magic items. Only one such item may be effectively 'worn' in each slot.
Head
: Circlets, headbands, helmets, tiaras, eyepatches, goggles, etc.
Neck
: Amulets, cloaks, mantles, and other jewelry and garments worn or fastened around the neck.
Torso
: Armor or other clothing; even if it is particularly bulky armor that would include leg armor or other pieces in addition to just a chestpiece, these collectively only take up the "torso" slot.
Hands
: Gloves, bracers, and other things worn on the hands or forearms (excluding rings).
Rings
: In addition to the hand slot, you may wear one ring on each hand you have. You may in theory wear more rings than this, but if you wear more than one Masterwork ring on a hand you are unable to benefit from any of them.
Held:
Characters with the standard number of limbs will have two hands with which to wield weapons, shields, and other items. A single item takes up a single hand unless it is listed as "2-handed", in which case you require both hands to wield it.
Feet
: Boots, sandals, footwraps, and other things you wear on your feet.

Masterwork Items
Masterwork items are the result of powerful magic, expert craftsmanship, and/or special materials. Items of this caliber have a variety of special powers and effects, and are highly sought after by many people. Listed for each item is its name, a description of what the item does, the equipment slot the item occupies, and a listed power - Common, Uncommon, Rare, and Extraordinary- that serves as a rough measure of both the potency of the power of the item as well as the price it will likely have.
If an item is a weapon or piece of armor, any restrictions on the type of weapon or armor will be listed in the description. Unless otherwise stated, by default a particular item can be created aany type of weapon or armor.

- 12

- 24

- 48
When the raw materials for a masterwork item are relevant, such as for item creation, half the cost accounts for mundane materials such as metal or wood and the other half accounts for rare materials that may be harder to find
(subject to GM approval, etc.)
.
Some enhancements provide effects of varying strengths, such as a numeric bonus, and some are generic powers that can take more specific forms, with regards to particular elements and the like. Masterwork items with specific powers may be referred to with numerals after the name to denote the strength of the bonus, and with generic terms replaced with the specific version that item has; such an item might be named 'Frost Warding Band II', for example, if it is a version of the Elemental Warding Band that provides 2 Frost Soak.

## Weapons and Armor  
*Source: `weapons-and-armor.html` (Part 2)*

These are the default weapons and armor that are available, although other items with different statistics might be available. Weapons and armor listed here do not refer to specific armaments but instead are listed in specific categories based on their general construction, purpose, and function.
Weapons occupy the Held slot and armor occupies the Torso slot, even if the armor's construction includes pieces worn on the arms or legs.
The items themselves are listed under
Adventuring Goods
.
Accuracy
: This applies as a modifier for all attack flips with the weapon.
Damage
: The amount of Physical damage the weapon deals on a successful hit with an attack.
Defense
: This applies as a modifier to Parry Defense when the weapon is used to Parry. Weapons with no Defense value cannot be used to Parry.
Range
: The range, in meters, for the weapon. Weapons that have a range of "C" are close-range weapons, and can only be used to attack adjacent targets. Other weapons are ranged weapons. Weapons with multiple ranges listed can be used as either close-range weapons or ranged weapons, using the relevant Skill.
You have Bad Luck on attacks with a ranged weapon against targets past its range increment. You cannot target a creature farther than twice a ranged weapon's range increment.
Relevant Skill
: The Skill(s) the weapon uses for attacking and determining Parry Defense. Melee and Brawl are only used to make close-range attacks, Acrobatics and Archery are used to make ranged attacks.
Hands Used
: Indicates if the weapon requires one or two hands to effectively fight with. A one-handed weapon occupies one Held slot, and a two-handed weapon occupies two Held slots. A two-handed weapon can be carried in only one hand if it is not being actively used to attack or Parry.
Might Skill Total Requirement
: The minimum Might Skill Total required to effectively use the armor or weapon. If you do not meet this requirement for an item, if it is armor then while wearing it you gain 1 less AP in combat, and if it is a weapon then any action using that weapon takes an extra 1 AP.
Physical Resist
: The amount of Physical Resist the armor provides while you are wearing it.
Agility Limit
: If your Agility is higher than this value while you wear the armor, you treat it as being this value for determining your Speed and Dodge Defense.

## Weapons and Armor  
*Source: `weapons-and-armor_1.html` (Part 1)*

These are the default weapons and armor that are used. There certainly might exist weapons or armor with statistics that differ from these, but this list is the default - stock weapon and armor options that are in balance relative to each other.
Weapons are broken into specific categories based on whether they are one- or two-handed, with what Skill they are used, and whether they are more finesse-based or power-based. These are not referring to specific weapons but instead roughly grouping them based on construction, how the weapon is wielded in a fight, and so on.
Armor is grouped even more loosely, simply indicating what level of protection the armor provides and what degree of encumbrance is entailed.  "Light" armor might include a thick leather chestpiece, a light chain tunic, and anything else with the same general caliber of armor. "Heavy" armor is going to be much bulkier, weigh the wearer down more, and provide a lot more protection.
Armor Soak
: The amount of Armor Soak the armor provides while you are wearing it.
Dexterity Limit
: While wearing the armor or using the shield, if your Dexterity is higher than this value is it reduced to this value for the purpose of your Speed, Dodge Defense, and Stealth.
Might Skill Total Requirement
: The minimum Might Skill Total required to effectively use the armor or weapon. If you do not meet this requirement, you suffer a penalty equal to the amount by which you fall short of the requirement. For armor, this applies as a penalty to Dexterity. For weapons, the penalty to all attack flips with it and halves the range.
Accuracy
: This applies as a modifier for all attack flips with the weapon.
Damage
: The amount of Physical damage the weapon deals on a successful hit with an attack.
Defense
: This applies as a modifier to Parry Defense when the weapon is used to Parry. Weapons with no Defense value cannot be used to Parry.
Range
: The range, in meters, for the weapon. Weapons that have a range of "C" are close-range weapons, and can only be used to attack adjacent targets. Weapons with a range of "Sx" are ranged weapons, and have a range of 'x' meters for each point of Strength you have. If you use a ranged weapon to attack a target beyond its range, you suffer a -2 penalty for each extra increment of its range your target is away from you.
Relevant Skill
: Skill(s) that can be used to wield the weapon in combat for either attacking or determining Parry Defense. Attacking with Melee or Martial Arts involves swinging or stabbing with the weapon at a target in close-range, attacking with Acrobatics is throwing the weapon at a target at range, and attacking with Archery uses the weapon to launch projectiles, such as arrows or bolts, at the target at range.
Hands Used
: Indicates if the weapon requires one or two hands to effectively fight with. A weapon that requires only one hand to wield is a one-handed weapon, and a weapon that requires two hands to wield is a two-handed weapon.
Notes
: Miscellaneous notes about the armor or weapon, extra functions or restrictions, etc.
