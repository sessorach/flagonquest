Okay so time to tie all the crafting together\.

First, __A NOTE ON GOLD COSTS AND VALUE__\. Gold is arbitrarily valued at ~1\.5 value per Gold, on a one\-time basis\. Masterwork Items with reusable powers cost a lot, but eventually make up for it by virtue of providing their value over and over\. Potions are very good, but kind of permanently lose the Gold in a sense, rather than the investment that builds up over time of focusing on Masterwork Items\. A good approach probably uses both? That’s the goal anyway\.

By extension, consumable items \(which always cost 2 Gold per Level\) are equal to a fixed Technique of the same level\. For example, Bottled Fire \(a Level 2 Grenade\) should basically be the same as a Level 2 Battle Magic Spell, with damage and range\. 

# <a id="_bshs7igs138s"></a>Crafting System

Main components:

- Skill: requires a Skill Total, as well as training in the specific School
- Materials: requires raw materials, as well as tools; some schools require additional equipment \(Smithing needs a forge\)
- Recipe: Need to know how to make the item in question; most basic items are included with tools, this is mostly a gatekeep for masterworks
- Time: It takes a length of time to finish the items\. This is to slightly restrict when they can be crafted, to help the game flow smoothly and limit this kind of thing to downtime activities\. Potential for a Technique to remove this restriction to give some flexibility, at least for some items\. Baseline is 8 hours, masterworks take another 8 hours \(so an entire MW item is a full day of crafting\)\.

So a crafting stat block looks like:

JEWELED SWORD

	__Base Item__: Any melee weapon

__School__: Smithing

	__Skill Total__: Craft 6

	__Total Materials__: 12

	__Base Materials__: Metal \(if Smithing\) or Wood \(if Carving\) or Stone \(if Carving\) or Bone \(if Carving\), and Precious

	__Extra Materials__: Leather, Cloth

Base Item is for Masterwork and other enhancements; you have to have those materials too\.

School is the training you need to make the item\. 

Skill Total is the minimum Skill Total you need in the listed Skill\.

Total Materials is the number of materials of the item’s Level or higher needed in order to create the item, taken from the Base \(and possibly Extra\) materials listed for the item\. 

Base Materials are required to make the item\. You need to use at least one material of each type listed for the item, though many items have requirements that can be met with one of a few different types \(if you have the matching School\)\. If a material has a number, that’s the minimum needed of that type\. 

Extra Materials are types that can be used to make up the item, but are not required\. Up to half of the item’s materials may be extra materials\.

Sample item crafting stat box:

__Base Item__: Any non\-shield weapon

__School__: \[Same as base item\]

__Skill Total__: \[Same as base item\] 6

__Total Materials__: 20

__Base Materials__: \[Same as base item\], 2 Fire, and 4 Shadow

# <a id="_nxc5r96hypc"></a>POISON RITUAL

How should poisons be valued? This ties into the general value of gold and items and all that, which sounds like a pain in the ass\. 

NVM, it turns out it’s super easy\. Each stack of a standard debuff like Crippled effectively is worth like 2\.5 points; \-1 effect, ~2\.5 enemy turns

Level 1 \(create DC 9\): Concentration \+2, Potency 1

Level 2 \(create DC 11\): Concentration \+4, Potency 2

Level 3 \(create DC 13\): Concentration \+6, Potency 3

Level 4 \(create DC 15\): Concentration \+8, Potency 4

Level 5 \(create DC 17\): Concentration \+10, Potency 5

# <a id="_e3o0xiwz08li"></a>THE GOLD STANDARD

Thus, the end result is that each poison is effectively “worth” a Technique of that Level\. So the Gold standard is that however much gold a consumable, one\-time effect costs relates to that\. 3 Gold per Level would mean Poisons cost 3/6/9/12/15, and their effects are basically that amount\. 

Extending this metric, 1 Gold literally \-> \+1 value\.

The basis for this is \+1 value as a one\-time effect, as opposed to Techniques and other things that are expected to be used 2x per day\.

Everything costed in Gold should have costs rounded up to express the versatility and convenience of spending Gold as opposed to committing Experience\.

## <a id="_86j894wlythq"></a>Permanent Items / Experience Purchases

For items and Experience \(vow of poverty etc\.\) and other things that represent a permanent investment of Gold, a certain pricing basis should be used\. For that we need a set of assumptions:

- Average campaign length: 12 sessions
- Average session length: 3\-4 hours, 2 combats, 1 noncombat \(encompasses all manner of stealth and social stuff\)

NVM it literally doesn’t matter\. Check this out:

No matter what you do with it, Gold and Experience are resources that you invest and get stuff out of\. Gold is one that you can spend on temporary things, though\. But, for both of them, it’s technically true that your earlier purchases provide more “value” over the course of the game\.

So, having equipment scale linearly makes sense\. Technically, sitting on a bunch of Gold to afford a cool Level 5 item is kind of a poor use of money, since it isn’t doing anything in the meantime\. However, that’s only true if you could get a lower\-level item in the meantime; the last half or whatever of that Gold isn’t going to get used earlier than you get it, regardless\.

Finally, being able to invest more Gold into a single slot in order to get a proportional benefit has value of its own\. You can get a cheap hat that gives \+1 Mental Defense or whatever, or you can save up and get a better one that gives \+2\. But, since you only have one slot, you can’t get a cheap hat then upgrade to a \+2 without losing some Gold\.

### <a id="_pg4gw57z1fk2"></a>__Recreational Drugs, Poor Quality \(Level 1\)__

*There are drugs that are simply naturally\-occurring plants that are smoked or ingested to provide a high, but there are also drugs that are manufactured by people\. Generally this manufacturing process involves alchemy to refine the base substance\(s\) and remove impurities, but for drugs of this quality much of that is\.\.\. overlooked\. Either some aspect of the refining process was done improperly \(or ignored\), the base materials were contaminated or too poor, or the drug being created is simply a dangerous and harmful substance\. Drugs of this "caliber" frequently form very strong habits, withdrawal can be lethal, and surviving long\-term use will see adverse effects from the continued abuse\.*

These drugs are referred to in doses\. A list of common side\-effects of various drugs of this caliber are:

- While under the effects of the drug, you are easily angered and tend to react to any adverse situation with violence\. In any situation where someone stops you from getting what you want you must make an Integrity flip, Difficulty 11, or at the very least threaten violence\. In the case that someone threatens you or otherwise suggests engaging in violence with you, you must spend 1 Focus or immediately attack them\.
- You are mentally unhinged to a certain degree, sometimes having difficulty distinguishing reality from\.\.\. something else\. You hear voices and phantom noises, catch visions of something otherworldly out of the corner of your eye, and sometimes you simply see something one moment only to blink and see a different scenario\. Even after ending use of the drug, this effect persists until you receive medical attention\.
- You immediately lose 1 Health that cannot be healed unless you are completely sobered from the influence of the drug\.
- For each month of continued use of the drug, your highest Health level has its maximum reduced by 1 until you go a month without using the drug \(possible suffering withdrawal\)\. This effect is cumulative with itself, and once a Health level has its maximum reduced to 0 your next highest Health level will begin to be affected\. If this reduced your Incapacitated Health level to a maximum of 0, you die\.

Material Type: Medicinal

### <a id="_cofey8z7saxm"></a>__Wagon, Large \(Level 1\)__

*A great wooden wagon, made from sturdy timber and bands of iron\. Wagons of these size generally have walls and are covered on top, with a bench for the driver and a cabin for passengers and cargo, and require a small team of pack animals to convey\. Designs for these wagons vary wildly between different cultures and civilizations, and can include features such as a loading ramp \(for cargo wagons\), two small cabins separated by a wall and with separate doors \(for passenger wagons\), walls that fold down into a small stage \(for thespians and other performers\), and many other designs\.*

Specifically, wagons of this size have enough room for several people and their belongings or a couple dozen barrels or crates of goods, assuming the interiors are not used by other features\.

# <a id="_c6bvjzu5uygw"></a>THE BIN

Cloak of Faces \(Level 5\)

An incredibly rare \(and some say distasteful\) item\. Usually made of rough leather, this cloak is heavy, large, and features a spacious hood\. 

You can place the cloak over the face of a recently\-deceased humanoid, and after a full round it will steal the corpse's face, leaving only bone and musculature underneath\. 

After wearing the cloak for a full day the wearer becomes attuned to the cloak, lasting until another wearer attunes to it\. While attuned, the wearer is aware of every face the cloak has ever stolen\. The wearer can spend 2 AP, raise the hood, and replace their face with any face that has been stolen by the cloak\. 

This causes the wearer's face to actually change features to that of the new face, and is not an illusion\. This effect lasts until the hood is lowered, reverting the wearer's face to their usual face\. 

Slot: Neck

Base Item: Basic clothing

Material Types: Leather and Shadow
