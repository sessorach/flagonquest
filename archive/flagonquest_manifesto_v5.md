Based on that the foraging expert should probably be pretty low level, but I’m inclined to bump it to Level 3 just because that’s free money you can stockpile for when you need\. There’s certainly a power multiplier to that, and it’s an incredibly rare ability that generates Gold\.

# <a id="_prp17zjh8b58"></a>Summoner’s Spliff

Ok so basic model is that summons don’t get AP as normal\. Instead, at any time, you can spend 2 AP to give them 4 AP, but they can only attack once per turn\. You can only do this once per round \(?\) per dude\. This allows them to use Interrupts, but you have to give them AP to do it\.

This does present some obvious balance considerations, notably that each summoning ability effectively grants an extra 2 AP each turn\. This can be balanced against the power of the summons, though; it’s that the player gets 2 AP, the player exchanges 2 for 4 from the summon\. This, in turn, presents the question of why a player would want to spend XP for the privilege of a summon with bad stats, instead of making an attack themselves\. Restricting Summons to 1 attack per turn will go a long way to smooth out extreme cases\.

Summons should be evaluated based on turns they get in a combat, how many enemy attacks they absorb, and so on\. 

- Summoned on turn 1, and used on turns 2\-4, 3 turns total for a good average
- We can consider that a Summon will have some method of scaling their stats with the Summoner so that their attacks are an extension of the Summoner
- Their attacks may have an added effect, or have access to an Interrupt, or something similar, which adds in
- Thus, each turn a Summon is active is a net gain of 1 AP on average \(Moving\), plus any abilities on hit, Interrupts, and so on
- They probably absorb an enemy attack as well, perhaps 1 on average per summoning

This would suggest that the average Summon exists for 3 turns, each of which gaining 1 AP, plus abilities, plus 1 enemy attack \(2 AP\) \- for a total of 5 AP \+ abilities, which is like 15 points plus abilities? That seems incredibly high\.

The abilities usually cost like 2 AP to cast, though, so that balances out them eating an enemy attack\. So really, just do like 3 turns per combat, and they gain baseline 1 AP to move into attack range / bait enemy into their range, but that probably doesn’t repeat? So the value is __1 AP \+ 3 \* \(total value of deviation from “average player stats” at that level\)__

Two Stat groupings for Summons:

	Primary \(Scale off of “damage stat”\): Damage, Health

	Secondary \(Scale off of “accuracy stat”\): Resist, Speed

	Skills \(Scale off of “skill total”\): Accuracy, Defenses

Adjustments to Damage, Accuracy, and Speed all happen once per turn, so 3x total over the course of an encounter\.

Immunities \(usually Mental/Bodily Defense\) require enemies to use alternate attacks sometimes, or otherwise immunize the summon from certain dangers\. This means that each of those immunities will force an enemy to not use that on them, happening 25% of the time, meaning the enemy targets a party member instead but there might not be good targets, so we can sort of eyeball this as being worth \+1 when it happens, and it would happen each of the ~3 rounds the summon is out, so let’s round up and call each immunity worth \+1 over the course of an encounter\.

# <a id="_4jb1tfnsgax1"></a>Card Suit Quest

Suits matter, should be interesting, make flips a bit more dynamic\. Side effect, this makes a decision to opt into more luck or try to control it\.

Fundamental rework of the system, in part, is necessary to really fulfill this\. Hopefully it’s as simple as possible, but this needs to basically just be an extra element\. Worked in seamlessly maybe, have to see\. 

The basic idea is: each action can benefit from Suits\. You count the suits of cards used for the action \- this includes the card flipped for the action, any cards from Good Luck that were also flipped, and any cards Played to support it\. Some actions that require cards be discarded to activate them *might* be able to include those, but under this model that probably shouldn’t be the norm since the value of a card in hand is basically for its ability to add suits anyway\.

So for example, if someone has 2x Good Luck and flips a Spade, Heart, and a Club, then plays a Heart for it, that action benefits from 1 Spade, 1 Club, 2 Hearts\.

Actions can then include this like it would any other stat\. An effect might deal 2 \+ \[Body \+ Hearts\] damage or something\. Any reference to card suits like that by default just counts the suits in the flip\.

	\*For enemies, cards played by the party instead *subtract* those Suits from the pool, with appropriate effects

So what do they do? Well, the idea should be that baseline no action inherently uses them; except damaging attacks, because whatever\. Something like all damaging attacks add \[Hearts \+ Clubs\] to damage\.

Might be good to give each suit a “baseline” effect, and then some additional things it affects\. There’s the breakdown of effects each maps to, but maybe if Hearts and Clubs add damage, Spades and Diamonds add to action results? So if you have \+5 to a skill, then flip a 7 of Spades, and play a Diamond, the result is 5 \+ 7 \+ 1 \+ 1 = 14\.

Things like Battle Strike get easy to work out, because you can add the suits to different effects in a simple matching way; if Clubs map to Crippled, then the feature for Battle Strike that Cripples enemies on hit applies X \+ \[Clubs\] stacks\. 

Unique abilities can be handled on a similar basis\. Maybe refine the symbolism list a bit more, then use that\. If Diamonds sort of represent Shadow, Cleverness, things like that, then it would make sense for stealthy abilities to scale off of those; if Vanish is a Stealth attack against enemies to hide in plain sight, then maybe the attack’s result adds \+\[Diamonds\] to it\. 

__THE GOAL FOR THIS IS__ that the suits should be about equal, on average, but distinct and interesting\.
